# NxtWave Growth Challenge
## Growth Plan: 500 Registrations in 7 Days

**Campaign:** Build Your First AI Project in 60 Minutes  
**Objective:** Generate 500 unique registrations from final-year engineering students within seven days.  
**Budget cap:** ₹2,000  
**Approach:** Campus-led distribution, referral attribution, and daily optimization.

### 1. Audience and insight

**Primary audience:** Final-year engineering students who are curious about AI but may lack confidence in turning concepts into a working project.

**Why they may care:** A practical, time-bounded introduction to AI offers a more approachable starting point than a broad course or an open-ended tutorial.

**Registration trigger:** A clear beginner-friendly promise, a free workshop, a specific 60-minute commitment, and a direct registration link shared by a trusted peer.

**Working hypothesis:** Relevant peer recommendations and low-friction registration will outperform broad, untargeted promotion on a limited budget. This is a hypothesis to validate, not an established result.

### 2. Channel strategy

**Priority 1 — Campus ambassadors**

Recruit or activate 25 student ambassadors across relevant engineering colleges and student communities. Give each ambassador a unique referral code, ready-to-share messages, and a simple daily target.

- Planning contribution: 25 × 12 = 300 registrations.
- Why this channel: Peer trust, access to relevant student networks, and trackable referral attribution.
- Execution: Share once in relevant groups where permitted, invite interested students personally, and follow up respectfully.

**Priority 2 — College communities**

Share through 20 relevant class groups, technical clubs, and student community channels, with administrator approval where required.

- Planning contribution: 20 × 10 = 200 direct registrations.
- Why this channel: One relevant community post can reach multiple eligible students without requiring paid advertising.
- Execution: Use a short message, the workshop's practical value proposition, and one clear registration link.

These contributions are planning assumptions. The channels must be monitored for overlap, and the final count must represent unique registrations rather than the sum of unverified estimates.

### 3. Seven-day execution plan

| Day | Main action | Decision or output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Validate registration flow, recruit ambassadors, prepare messaging | Working funnel and initial distribution network |
| 2 | Activate ambassadors and first college communities | First registrations and feedback |
| 3 | Review referral performance and common questions | Identify high-response messages and channels |
| 4 | Expand the best-performing outreach; re-engage inactive ambassadors | Improve distribution efficiency |
| 5 | Compare actual progress with the cumulative target | Reallocate effort to underperforming channels |
| 6 | Focus on high-intent communities and unanswered questions | Close the remaining target gap |
| 7 | Final relevant reminders, verify records, deduplicate | Confirm unique registrations and document results |

### Daily registration pacing

The cumulative targets below are operating checkpoints, not predictions based on historical conversion data.

| Day | Daily target | Cumulative target |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 25 | 25 |
| 2 | 50 | 75 |
| 3 | 75 | 150 |
| 4 | 90 | 240 |
| 5 | 100 | 340 |
| 6 | 100 | 440 |
| 7 | 60 | 500 |
| **Total** | **500** | **500** |

**Adjustment rule:** At the end of each day, compare actual unique registrations with the cumulative checkpoint. If behind target, review registrations by source and ambassador, identify the strongest channels, and shift the next day's outreach toward those channels. Do not increase message frequency indiscriminately; prioritize relevant audiences and respectful follow-up.


### 4. Registration forecast and measurement

| Source | Forecast | Share of target |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ambassador-attributed registrations | 300 | 60% |
| Direct community registrations | 200 | 40% |
| **Total** | **500** | **100%** |

**Budget and attribution controls:** A registration with a valid referral code is attributed to its ambassador; a registration without one is classified as direct. Deduplicate using the unique email constraint. Track daily registrations, source, ambassador performance, and remaining registrations.

**Daily operating rule:** Compare actual registrations with the cumulative plan, identify the weakest-performing channel, and move effort toward channels showing stronger evidence of qualified interest.

### 5. Budget and safeguards

### Budget allocation

| Activity | Budget | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Campus ambassador coordination | ₹600 | Communication and coordination across participating campuses |
| Campus activation materials | ₹500 | Simple digital creatives and limited printable materials |
| High-performing channel support | ₹600 | Reallocate toward channels showing stronger registration results |
| Contingency | ₹300 | Cover small unexpected campaign costs |
| **Total** | **₹2,000** | |

**Budget discipline:** Start with free channels—student communities, ambassador networks, and shareable digital creatives. Spend only where there is a clear distribution need, and reallocate based on observed results. The ₹2,000 allocation is a proposed budget, not money already spent.

### Success criteria

- 500 unique, eligible registrations by the end of Day 7.
- Referral attribution and duplicate handling work correctly.
- Daily progress and channel performance can be reviewed.
- Spending stays within ₹2,000.
- Outreach is relevant, respectful, and compliant with community rules.

**Current status:** The registration and analytics prototype is deployed. The campaign has not been executed; the 500-registration goal remains a target, not a result.
