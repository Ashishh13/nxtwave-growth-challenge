# AI Learning Notes — NxtWave Growth Challenge

## 1. AI helped turn the campaign idea into a measurable plan

**Prompt (paraphrased):** How can I plan for 500 final-year engineering student registrations in seven days with a ₹2,000 budget?

**AI suggestion:** Use multiple outreach channels and define targets, a daily schedule, and a budget.

**What I changed:** I narrowed the plan to two priority channels: campus ambassadors (300 projected registrations) and college communities (200 projected registrations). I added daily cumulative checkpoints and a rule to shift effort toward channels producing actual registrations.

**What I learned:** A list of marketing ideas is less useful than a focused plan with measurable assumptions and a way to adapt.

## 2. AI helped me build a working growth asset

**Prompt (paraphrased):** How can I make the campaign plan demonstrable through a working prototype rather than submitting only a document?

**AI suggestion:** Build a registration and referral-tracking application with a dashboard.

**What I changed:** I implemented a Streamlit prototype connected to Supabase. It supports student registration, campus ambassador referral codes, and campaign analytics. I also deployed the app so it can be demonstrated through a live link.

**What I learned:** A prototype makes the strategy easier to inspect, but a functioning application does not prove that the marketing campaign will achieve its target. Actual reach and conversion still need testing.

## 3. A decision I made instead of blindly following AI

**Prompt (paraphrased):** How can I distribute the workshop and increase registrations quickly?

**Possible AI suggestion to document, if this matches your actual interaction:** Use broad, high-volume outreach to maximize reach quickly.

**My decision:** I prioritized relevant student communities and trusted campus networks instead of treating mass messaging as the default. I included guidance to avoid misleading claims and repeated unwanted messages.

**Why:** High message volume does not guarantee qualified registrations. Irrelevant outreach can annoy students and damage trust.

**What I learned:** AI can generate options, but I need to evaluate them against the audience, budget, time available, and ethical constraints.

## What I would improve with 24 more hours

- Pilot the prototype with a small group and test the complete registration journey.
- Validate the channel assumptions with real outreach and track unique registrations by source.
- Add basic abuse protection and rate limiting before broader public use.
- Refine the plan using observed conversion and cost data instead of assumptions.

## Honesty note

This challenge was planned as a simulation; the campaign was not executed. The 500-registration figure is a target, not an achieved result. The channel estimates and budget are proposals, not validated outcomes. The examples above should be edited to match my actual AI interactions and decisions.
